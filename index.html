01 · Getting around

Move

Bến Thành is the centre of everything. Most of old Saigon is a 5–20 minute walk; for anything further, open an app rather than flagging a street taxi.

🛵Ride apps — Grab, Xanh SM, BeYour default for anything beyond walking

Price is fixed up front, so no haggling or meter surprises. Pay cash to the driver or link a card in the app.

  • Bike (xe ôm) — fastest through traffic, cheapest. Driver gives you a helmet. Hold on, keep bags in front.
  • Car — better in rain, heat, with luggage, or for longer hops like RMIT.
  • Xanh SM — the teal/green electric VinFast cars & bikes. Clean, quiet, often the nicest ride.
  • Always check the plate number before you get on. Drop the pickup pin precisely — drivers often can't find hotel lobbies.
Short hop in D1 (bike)15–35k ₫ Across town (car)60–150k ₫ To RMIT Saigon South (car)120–220k ₫
🚇Metro Line 1Starts under Bến Thành Market — your doorstep

Saigon's first metro line (opened Dec 2024) runs from Bến Thành east to Suối Tiên. Clean, air-conditioned, and traffic-proof.

  • Thảo Điền station — expat cafés, brunch & vegan spots (~15 min).
  • Văn Thánh / Tân Cảng — closest stops for Landmark 81.
  • Nhà hát Thành phố (Opera House) — one stop, handy in the rain.

Single trips are roughly 7k–20k ₫ and day passes exist. Buy at the ticket machines or ask at the counter; contactless cards/QR work at many gates. Runs roughly early morning to ~10pm — check at the station.

🚶Walking & crossing the streetThe one skill you need on day one
  1. Look for a gap — not an empty road, it won't come.
  2. Walk at a slow, steady, predictable pace. Bikes flow around you.
  3. Don't stop suddenly, don't run, don't step back.
  4. Watch cars and buses — they won't swerve. Raise a hand if it helps you feel seen.

Sidewalks double as motorbike parking and kitchens, so you'll weave a lot. Walk in the morning or after 4pm to dodge the heat.

✈️Airport ↔ Bến ThànhTân Sơn Nhất (SGN), ~7 km
  • Grab / Xanh SM car — follow the app's pickup signs in arrivals. 30–60 min depending on traffic, roughly 120–250k ₫.
  • Bus 109 — yellow airport bus to the 23/9 Park terminal right next to Bến Thành. Cheap and air-conditioned; good if you're travelling light.
  • Ignore touts in the arrivals hall offering "taxi".

Double-check which airport is on your ticket — the new Long Thành airport is ~40 km east and needs 1h+ by car.

⛴️Saigon WaterbusThe city from the river, cheaply

Leaves from Bạch Đằng pier (end of Nguyễn Huệ, ~15 min walk). Not fast transport — think of it as a cheap river cruise. Go for the late-afternoon run to catch sunset over the skyline.

Schedules change; check at the pier or on Google Maps. Map →

🚕Taxis & cyclosFine with care
  • If you must hail one: Vinasun (white) or Mai Linh (green) only, meter on. Watch for look-alike logos.
  • Cyclos are a tourist experience, not transport. Agree price and duration first — write it down on your phone and show it.

02 · Plant-based in Saigon

Eat

Chay means vegetarian, thuần chay means vegan. Saigon has a deep Buddhist vegetarian tradition, so meat-free is easy. The traps are hidden fish sauce, egg and condensed milk.

Words to watch for

nước mắmfish sauce mắm tômshrimp paste dầu hàooyster sauce trứngegg — common even in chay places sữa đặccondensed milk bơ / patebutter / pâté (bánh mì) mỡ hànhscallions in pork fat chà bôngmeat floss (on xôi) mật onghoney

Safe swap: ask for nước tương (soy sauce) instead of fish sauce. Mushroom "chà bông" in chay shops is fine.

Dishes to hunt down

Phở chay

Veggie broth noodle soup with tofu & mushrooms. Load up on herbs and lime.

Bánh mì chay

Baguette with tofu, mock meat, pickles, herbs. Say không bơ, không pate.

Gỏi cuốn chay

Fresh rice-paper rolls with a peanut–hoisin dip.

Bánh xèo chay

Crispy turmeric crêpe, wrapped in lettuce & herbs. Check the batter has no egg.

Bún Huế chay

Spicy lemongrass noodle soup, Huế-style.

Cơm chay

Point-and-pick rice plates at quán cơm chay — cheap, fast, tons of choice.

Rau muống xào tỏi

Morning glory stir-fried with garlic. Ask: không nước mắm, không dầu hào.

Chè

Sweet soups with beans, jelly & coconut milk — usually vegan by default.

Nước mía

Fresh sugarcane juice with kumquat. Perfect after a hot walk.

Coffee

Cà phê đen đá = iced black. Most milk coffee & coconut coffee uses condensed milk; modern cafés often have oat milk (sữa yến mạch).

🛒Groceries & snacksFor the room, the desk, the long days
  • Fruit — buy from market stalls: mango, dragon fruit, pomelo, rambutan, jackfruit. Ask them to cut it.
  • Fresh tofu & soy milk (đậu hũ, sữa đậu nành) — street carts in the morning.
  • Supermarkets — Co.opmart, Bách Hóa Xanh, or Annam Gourmet for imported vegan basics.
  • Convenience stores — Circle K / GS25 / FamilyMart are everywhere for water and snacks.

Day trips

  • Củ Chi Tunnels

    Wartime tunnel network ~1.5–2h away. Bến Đình is less crowded than Bến Dược. Often combined with the Cao Đài temple in Tây Ninh.

  • Mekong Delta

    Boats through coconut-palm canals, fruit orchards, rice-paper workshops (Mỹ Tho / Bến Tre, ~2h).

  • Cần Giờ Mangroves

    UNESCO biosphere reserve with mangrove forest and cheeky monkeys. Quieter, greener alternative.

Book through Klook / GetYourGuide or the hotel. When booking, request a vegan lunch in writing — most tours include a set meal.

04 · Practical stuff

Notes

💱 Money converter

=

Rate is approximate.

💵MoneyCash still rules at street level
  • Carry cash for street food, markets and small cafés. Cards work in restaurants, malls and hotels.
  • The 20k and 500k notes are both blue — check before you hand one over. Same trap with 10k/200k.
  • Prices are often written without zeros: "50k" or "50" means 50,000 ₫.
  • Use bank ATMs (Vietcombank, Techcombank, TPBank) inside malls or banks; choose to be charged in VND.
  • Tipping isn't expected. Rounding up, or 20–50k for great service or a massage, is appreciated.
📶Phone & data
  • Get a tourist SIM at airport arrivals (Viettel has the best coverage) or an eSIM before you fly. You'll need your passport.
  • Wi-Fi is everywhere — the password is usually on the wall or the receipt.
  • Locals message on Zalo — handy if a host or driver asks for it.
🌦️Weather & what to wear
  • Hot and humid year-round, roughly 24–34°C. Early November is the tail of the rainy season — expect a short, heavy afternoon downpour, then sun.
  • Pack: light breathable clothes, a compact rain jacket, sunscreen, mosquito repellent, sandals you can get wet.
  • Temples & pagodas: cover shoulders and knees; remove shoes where you see others do it.
  • Malls, metro and offices are icy with AC — a thin layer helps.
🛡️Safety & scams
  • Phone snatching from passing bikes is the main risk. Use your phone away from the road edge; crossbody bags worn on the inside.
  • Coconut / fruit-pole "photo" vendors and shoe shiners will ask for money after the fact — a firm, friendly không, cảm ơn.
  • Confirm prices before you sit, eat or ride when nothing is written down.
  • Saigon is generally very safe for walking at night in central areas.
💧Health
  • Don't drink tap water. Bottled is everywhere; ice in restaurants and cafés is factory-made and fine.
  • Pharmacies (nhà thuốc) are on every block — Long Châu and Pharmacity are reliable chains.
  • International-standard clinics: Family Medical Practice, Raffles Medical, and FV Hospital (near RMIT).
  • Hydrate with electrolytes on big walking days — the heat sneaks up on you.
🗺️Addresses & "districts"

Since July 2025, HCMC scrapped its district level and merged with neighbouring provinces, so new addresses use ward names (e.g. Phường Bến Thành). Locals, drivers and most signs still say "District 1", "District 7" etc. — both will get you there.

Tip: send drivers a Google Maps pin rather than typing an address.

🎓Work days at RMIT
  • Saigon South campus — 702 Nguyễn Văn Linh, Phú Mỹ Hưng (old District 7). About 30–50 min from Bến Thành by car.
  • Book a Grab/Xanh SM car 10 min before you need it in peak hours, and leave buffer for afternoon rain.
  • Phú Mỹ Hưng is calm, walkable and full of food options — handy for lunch or a post-work dinner before heading back.
