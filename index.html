Before you go

Entry & visaCheck this first
  • Check whether your passport needs an e-visa. If it does, apply only on the official government portal, evisa.gov.vn, and avoid look-alike agency sites.
  • Your passport needs 6+ months of validity and a blank page.
  • Keep a PDF of the e-visa and your booking on your phone, and a printout too.
MoneyCash still rules at street level
  • Carry cash for street food, markets and small cafés. Cards work in restaurants, malls and hotels.
  • The 20k and 500k notes are both blue, so check before you hand one over. The 10k and 200k notes are easy to mix up too.
  • "50k" or just "50" on a price means 50,000 ₫.
  • Use bank ATMs (Vietcombank, Techcombank) and choose to be charged in VND.
  • Tipping isn't expected, but rounding up for great service is appreciated.
Phone & dataeSIM or airport SIM
  • Buy an eSIM before you fly, or a tourist SIM in airport arrivals (Viettel has the best coverage). You'll need your passport.
  • Wi-Fi is everywhere, and the password is usually on the wall or your receipt.

Getting around

Move

Walk the old town. Use an app for anything further.

Ride appsGrab, Xanh SM, Be — your default

The price is fixed up front, so there's no haggling and no meter. Pay the driver in cash or link a card in the app.

  • Bike (xe ôm): fastest through traffic and cheapest. The driver gives you a helmet.
  • Car: better in rain or heat, or for longer trips like RMIT.
  • Xanh SM: the teal electric VinFast fleet, clean and quiet.
  • Always check the plate number, and drop the pickup pin precisely.

Saigon's first metro line runs from Bến Thành east to Suối Tiên. It's clean, air-conditioned and avoids the traffic.

  • Thảo Điền: cafés and vegan spots, about 15 min.
  • Tân Cảng: the closest stop for Landmark 81.
  • Nhà hát Thành phố (Opera House): your nearest station, about 7 minutes' walk up Nguyễn Huệ.

Crossing the streetThe one skill you need on day one
  1. Wait for a gap, not an empty road. The road won't empty.
  2. Walk at a slow, steady, predictable pace. Bikes will flow around you.
  3. Don't stop suddenly, run, or step back.
  4. Watch for cars and buses, because they won't swerve.
Airport ↔ hotelTân Sơn Nhất (SGN), ~7 km
  • Grab / Xanh SM car: follow the app's pickup signs in arrivals. It takes 30–60 min and costs roughly 120–250k ₫.
  • Bus 109: the airport bus to the 23/9 Park terminal by Bến Thành, then a 15-minute walk or a short Grab to the hotel. Cheap and air-conditioned.
  • Hotel pickup: the hotel can arrange one. Ask for the price when you book.
  • Ignore the touts offering a "taxi" in the arrivals hall.

Check your ticket: Long Thành airport is ~40 km east.

Taxis & cyclosFine, with care
  • If you hail one, use only Vinasun (white) or Mai Linh (green) and make sure the meter is on.
  • Cyclos are a tourist experience. Agree on the price and how long the ride lasts first, in writing.
Getting to RMITSaigon South campus
  • The campus is at 702 Nguyễn Văn Linh, Phú Mỹ Hưng (formerly District 7), 30–50 min by car.
  • At rush hour (7–9am and 4:30–7pm), book your car 10 minutes before you need it.
  • Phú Mỹ Hưng is calm and walkable, with lots of places for lunch.

Watch out for

The big three: keep your phone away from the road, use ride apps instead of street taxis, and drink far more water than you think.

Phone & bag snatchingThe most common crime
  • Thieves on motorbikes grab phones and bags from the kerb side, especially on busy streets and at night.
  • Walk with your phone on the building side, wear bags across your body away from the road, and step into a doorway to check Maps.
  • On a bike taxi, keep your phone in a zipped pocket. Don't hold it out to film.
Scams & overchargingTouts, fakes and add-ons
  • Fake taxis copy the Vinasun and Mai Linh colours and logos. Use an app, or check the name and phone number printed on the car.
  • A street xe ôm offering a ride "for 50" may ask for 500k at the end. Use Grab or Xanh SM.
  • Shoe shiners and fruit-pole sellers may start work or hand you the pole for a photo, then ask for money. Keep walking with a smile.
  • Cyclo drivers sometimes change the price halfway. Agree on the price, route and time in writing first.
  • If a ride-app driver asks you to cancel and pay cash, say no and stick to the app's price.
  • Spas and massages may press hard for a big tip or add "extras". Agree on the full price first.
Money mix-upsNotes that look alike
  • The 20k and 500k notes are both blue, and 10k and 200k look alike too. Count slowly, and keep big notes separate from small ones.
  • Check your change, and look at the bottom line of the bill, as service charge and VAT may be added.
  • Cards often carry a 2–3% fee. When a card machine or ATM offers to charge you in your home currency, choose VND instead.
  • Use ATMs inside banks or malls, and cover the keypad.
TrafficBikes go everywhere
  • Motorbikes ride on footpaths, the wrong way down one-way streets and through red lights. Look both ways, every time.
  • Don't hire a motorbike. You need a licence that's valid in Vietnam, and travel insurance usually won't cover you without one.
  • On a bike taxi, wear the helmet with the strap done up, and keep your legs clear of the exhaust pipe. Burns from it are common.
Heat, sun & mosquitoesThe everyday risk
  • Expect about 31° and humid. Drink water all day, and use oral rehydration salts (Oresol, from any pharmacy) after a long day out.
  • The UV is very high, even when it's cloudy. Wear sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses, and stay out of the midday sun.
  • Dengue fever is spread by mosquitoes that bite during the day. Use a DEET or picaridin repellent, especially in parks. See a doctor if you get a high fever with headache or joint pain.
Laws & rulesWhat gets tourists in trouble
  • Vapes and e-cigarettes are banned, including for tourists, and you can be fined. Leave them at home.
  • Penalties for drugs are severe, even for small amounts.
  • Don't photograph military sites or police, and don't fly a drone without a permit.
  • Keep a photo of your passport and visa on your phone. The hotel will record your passport at check-in.
  • At temples and the palace, cover your shoulders and knees, and take your shoes off where you see others have.
Floods & stormsHigh tides near the river
  • Late in the year, very high tides (triều cường) can flood low streets near the river for an hour or two in the evening, especially after rain. The hotel is close to the river, so wear sandals if the streets look wet.
  • Storm season is ending by December. If a typhoon is in the news, expect flight delays and check with your airline.

If something goes wrong

Phone or bag stolenLock it, then report it
  • Lock or erase the phone with Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android), and call your bank to block any cards.
  • For an insurance claim you need a police report from the local ward police station (công an phường). The hotel front desk can help with translation.
Lost passportAllow several days
  • Get a police report first, then contact your embassy or consulate for an emergency passport.
  • You'll also need a new visa or exit permit from the Immigration Department before you can fly. Tell your airline early.
Sick or hurtClinics with English-speaking staff
  • Minor problems: a pharmacy (nhà thuốc) can help with stomach upsets, colds and bites.
  • For a doctor, international clinics such as Family Medical Practice and Raffles Medical see walk-in patients. FV Hospital has a 24-hour emergency department.
  • Call your travel insurer's emergency line before any big treatment, as they can pay the hospital directly.

Australian Consulate-General

Level 20, Vincom Center, 47 Lý Tự Trọng, D1. Open weekdays in office hours. Out of hours, call the 24-hour Consular Emergency Centre.

Good to know

HealthWater, pharmacies, clinics
  • Don't drink tap water. Ice in restaurants and cafés is factory-made and fine.
  • There are pharmacies (nhà thuốc) on every block, such as Long Châu and Pharmacity.
  • International clinics include Family Medical Practice, Raffles Medical, and FV Hospital (near RMIT).
AddressesThe 2025 renaming

In 2025, HCMC replaced districts with wards, so the hotel's Phường Bến Nghé is now part of Phường Sài Gòn, and you'll see both on maps and receipts. Locals still say "District 1", and either works. The easiest way to give a driver an address is to send a Google Maps pin.
